Why I Started Running

I didn't know where I was going, but I had to start somewhere.

By Roger Jackson - November 10, 2026

I never grew up running. In fact, I hated it. The only reason I ever ran growing up was to play football or basketball. I remember running up and down the football field or doing sprints back and forth across the basketball court, and it was horrible. Running was just something I had to suffer through to play the sports I actually enjoyed. And let's be honest, I wasn't some incredible athlete, so my sports career ended after my senior year of high school. When that happened, so did my running. I never ran again, and I was perfectly fine with that.

Fast forward to December 2024. I'm 36 years old, out of shape, overweight, and somehow I get talked into joining this thing called F3. For those who don't know, F3 is a group of men working to become better leaders, better husbands, better fathers, and better men for their families, friends, and communities. It just happens to be disguised as a workout group. And let me tell you, those workouts used to absolutely destroy me. The worst part, you might ask? Cardio. More specifically, running. Suddenly, all those memories of running football fields and basketball courts came flooding back, except now I was 20 years older and about 100 pounds heavier. I couldn't do it. Running a quarter of a mile felt like running a marathon, and I dreaded it every single time.

In April 2025, I started thinking that I needed to do something to make these workouts more manageable. I was tired of being afraid of cardio. I was tired of being so exhausted from running that I barely had anything left for the rest of the workout. So I set a goal. I wanted to get to a point where I could run a quarter of a mile without stopping. That was it. I figured if I could do that, I'd be in pretty good shape for most of our F3 workouts. We occasionally ran farther, but most of the time, a quarter of a mile was about as much as we had to do at once.

So I started going to the track at my kids' school. Run the straights, walk the turns. That was my training plan. Nothing fancy, nothing impressive, just showing up and trying to get a little better. I did that for a few months, and when July came around, I started eating a little better, too. I figured if I was putting in all this work, I might as well start making some changes in other areas of my life. Through all of this, the guys at F3 were incredible. They encouraged me, pushed me, and ran alongside me, even when my pace was what I considered painfully slow. They never made me feel like I didn't belong, and that encouragement meant more than they probably realized.

Then came one morning in August that changed everything. I was out running with a good friend, and by this point, I had worked my way up to running about six-tenths of a mile without stopping. There was a sign along our route that I knew marked roughly 0.6 miles, and that sign had become my finish line. But that morning, when we reached it, something in me said, "Come on, let's go a little longer." So I did. And then I kept going. Before I knew it, I had run an entire mile without stopping. One whole mile! For some people, that's nothing, but for me, it was everything. I remember stopping and telling my friend, "I've never done that before." He was so excited for me, genuinely happy and proud. I don't think either of us realized just how much that moment would change my life, because that was the moment I caught the running bug. But even more than that, it was the moment something unlocked in my mind.

I had spent so much of my life telling myself there were things I physically couldn't do, and that morning I started to realize something. For years, my mind had been telling me my body couldn't go any farther, and I had mistaken that voice for my body's actual limit. Meanwhile, my body was somewhere in the background saying, "Hey, I think I've got a little more left." But I couldn't hear it over the voice in my head telling me to stop. That one mile taught me something I hadn't fully understood before. Sometimes the limits we place on ourselves aren't really our limits at all. Now, don't get me wrong, our bodies have real limits, and there's a difference between pushing through discomfort and ignoring something that's wrong. But I had spent years assuming I couldn't do things without ever really giving myself the chance to find out.

So, like Forrest Gump, I just kept running. First, I wanted to run a 5K. Then a 10K. Then 10 miles. Then I wanted to run a 5K in under 30 minutes and a 10K in under an hour. Every time I reached a goal, I started wondering what else might be possible. And then, in April 2026, I ran my first half marathon. 13.1 miles! The same guy who couldn't run a quarter of a mile without stopping had just run a half marathon. I couldn't believe it. But apparently, that wasn't enough, because shortly afterward, I signed up for a full marathon. And in September 2026, I did it. I ran 26.2 miles. I ran a marathon! Even writing those words feels a little crazy. These were things that, even at the beginning of 2026, I would have told you were impossible for me. And yet, here I am. The guy who hated running, who avoided cardio at all costs, and who thought a quarter of a mile was too much had just run a marathon. Somewhere along the way, I fell in love with the very thing I spent most of my life avoiding.

I don't know what's next for me with running. Maybe I'll get faster, maybe I'll run another marathon, or maybe I'll chase distances and goals I haven't even thought of yet. And honestly, I don't know what's next for me in other areas of life, either. But I do know that this journey has taught me something I'll carry with me forever. We are often capable of so much more than we give ourselves credit for. Sometimes all it takes is showing up, starting small, being consistent, surrounding yourself with people who believe in you, and being willing to challenge that voice in your head that says you can't. Not every limit is meant to be pushed through, but some of the limits we live with are ones we've placed on ourselves, and you'll never know the difference if you're too afraid to try.

I'm still learning, still growing, and still finding out what I'm capable of. But if there's one thing this journey has taught me, it's this: Don't be so quick to decide what you can't do. You might just surprise yourself. I know i sure did!
